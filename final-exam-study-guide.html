Cardiovascular

Covered in all three courses, so it's the heaviest-weighted system. Dive deeper: NUR 325 HLD & HTN · NUR 325 coagulation · NUR 326 cardiovascular · NUR 406 Week 1

TopicHigh-yield points
RAAS & BP drugsRenin → angiotensin I → ACE (lungs) → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction + aldosterone: sodium and water retained). ACE inhibitors: dry cough (top reason to switch), angioedema, hyperkalemia, not in pregnancy. ARBs ("-sartan") block angiotensin II. Beta blockers ("-olol") lower HR and contractility. CCBs dilate arterioles; verapamil and diltiazem also slow HR and conduction.
Lipids & statinsLDL goal below 130 (below 70 if high risk). Statins: myopathy, rhabdomyolysis (AKI), hepatotoxicity (AST/ALT).
Stable angina vs ACSStable: exertion, relieved by rest, 2–5 min. ACS = unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI ("time is tissue"); pain lasts longer, not relieved by nitroglycerin; women may report heartburn. First-time chest pain: call 911, never drive. Unstable angina: ECG changes return to normal, typically no troponin rise. MI: troponin trends up, ECG changes stay; STEMI = ST elevation, larger infarct. LAD = "widow-maker".
Acute MI drugsVS before every drug. Aspirin: chew the first dose (lowers mortality). Morphine: respiratory depression, hypotension. Nitroglycerin: lowers preload and afterload, doesn't lower mortality, never with sildenafil. Beta blockers lower O2 demand. PCI first; alteplase if no cath lab (bleeding is the main risk). Watch for reperfusion dysrhythmias (VTach/VFib).
Pericarditis & tamponadePericarditis: sharp pain worse with deep breaths, fever, friction rub; NSAIDs (colchicine if not working). Tamponade (emergency): hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds, pulsus paradoxus: pericardiocentesis.
Heart failureLeft = lungs (crackles, frothy sputum, orthopnea; usually from HTN). Right = rest of the body (JVD, dependent edema, weight gain, ascites; often from COPD). HF drugs: ACE/ARB/ARNI + beta blocker + spironolactone + SGLT2 inhibitor; watch potassium and kidneys.
DigoxinApical pulse 1 full minute, hold below 60. Toxicity risk: older age, women, diuretics, low potassium (the NCLEX favorite). Signs: bradycardia, confusion, nausea, visual changes. Antidote: digoxin immune Fab.
DysrhythmiasA-fib: irregularly irregular, no P waves; stroke risk (often anticoagulated). New onset: check electrolytes and O2 first. Adenosine for SVT: 6 mg rapid IV push then 12, then 12, with an immediate rapid flush; brief asystole expected; pads on. Amiodarone for almost any dysrhythmia (long half-life, many adverse effects).
Aneurysm & dissectionMostly from hypertension. Suspected AAA: light palpation only. Dissection: sudden severe tearing pain; surgical emergency; beta blockers slow tearing.
PAD vs VTEPAD: 5 P's (pain, pulselessness, palpable coolness, paresthesias, paresis); smoking cessation is the biggest treatment. Virchow's triad: venous stasis, endothelial damage, hypercoagulability.
AnticoagulantsHeparin → protamine. Warfarin → vitamin K; keep vitamin K foods consistent, not avoided. Platelets below 100,000 on heparin with new clots = HIT: stop heparin.

Respiratory & Acid-Base

Dive deeper: NUR 326 respiratory · NUR 406 acid-base & ABGs

TopicHigh-yield points
ABG stepspH (7.40 midpoint) → PaCO2 → HCO3 → compensation. The value that matches the pH is the problem. ROME: Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal. Uncompensated = one abnormal value; partial = both abnormal and pH abnormal; compensated = pH normal.
The four disordersResp acidosis: hypoventilation, overdose, sedation, COPD. Resp alkalosis: hyperventilation, panic, fever. Metabolic acidosis: renal failure, DKA, diarrhea, lactic acid (Kussmaul breathing, hyperkalemia). Metabolic alkalosis: vomiting, NG suction, loop diuretics, antacids.
Acidosis & potassiumIn acidosis, hydrogen moves into cells and potassium moves out (hyperkalemia). Buffers act in seconds, lungs in minutes to hours, kidneys in hours to days.
Asthma drugsThe only rescue drug is the SABA (albuterol); everything else is long-term control. Give the bronchodilator before the inhaled steroid, and rinse the mouth after inhaled steroids.
TuberculosisAirborne; latent (no symptoms) vs active (night sweats most suggestive, low-grade afternoon fever, weight loss, hemoptysis later). Contagious until sputum stops growing mycobacteria. Isoniazid: peripheral neuropathy (give B6), hepatotoxicity. Rifampin: harmless red-orange body fluids; CYP inducer.

Fluids & Electrolytes

Dive deeper: NUR 325 fluids & electrolytes

ImbalanceHigh-yield points
Hypokalemia (below 3.5)GI or renal losses (vomiting, diarrhea, furosemide), alkalosis. Dysrhythmias (can be lethal), weakness, leg cramps, slowed bowel. Raises digoxin toxicity risk.
Hyperkalemia (above 5.0)Renal failure, cell injury (burns, crush, sepsis), potassium-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, NSAIDs, acidosis, Addison's. Peaked T waves, weakness, cramps, diarrhea.
Hyponatremia (below 136)GI losses, diuretics, adrenal insufficiency, water intoxication. Brain cells swell: confusion.
Hypocalcemia (below 9.0)Hypoparathyroidism, pancreatitis, thyroid or parathyroid surgery (sudden drop: monitor closely). Increased neuromuscular excitability.

Neurologic

Dive deeper: NUR 325 dementia, pain & seizures · NUR 406 Neuro I · NUR 406 Neuro II

TopicHigh-yield points
Stroke87% ischemic, 13% hemorrhagic. Non-contrast CT first (blood is white): never anticoagulate a bleed. BE FAST; last known normal. Ischemic: alteplase (watch BP, HR, rhythm, intracranial bleeding) or thrombectomy. Hemorrhagic: reverse anticoagulation, control BP and ICP (HOB up, neutral head). Swallow study before food. Hemorrhagic: "worst headache of their life".
TIAUnder 24 h, no infarction, no residual effects; stroke risk highest in the first month. Dysphagia (swallowing: NPO) vs dysphasia (speech).
SeizuresPhenytoin: level 10–20; gingival hyperplasia (very good dental care). Valproic acid: hepatotoxic; works for every seizure type.
Parkinson'sLow dopamine, relative excess acetylcholine. Rest (pill-rolling) tremor, cogwheel rigidity, bradykinesia (most disabling), shuffling gait. Sinemet (carbidopa-levodopa) = gold standard: avoid high protein, orthostatic hypotension, dyskinesias, on-off effect.
Myasthenia gravisAutoimmune attack on ACh receptors; weakness that improves with rest; eyes and face first; aspiration and respiratory risk. Myasthenic crisis = too little drug (improves with edrophonium). Cholinergic crisis = too much (SLUDGE, bradycardia; worsens with edrophonium; atropine).
Multiple sclerosisT cells attack CNS myelin; women 20–40; relapsing-remitting most common. Paresthesias, optic neuritis, fatigue, bladder problems. Relapse: pulse IV steroids (watch glucose). Natalizumab: PML risk.
GBS & ALSGBS: ascending weakness after a viral infection; watch the breathing muscles (ventilator); 75% return to baseline. ALS: upper and lower motor neurons; death from respiratory failure; riluzole (hepatotoxic).
MeningitisTriad: fever, headache, nuchal rigidity; photophobia; petechial rash (meningococcal); Kernig and Brudzinski. Bacterial kills in hours: antibiotics ASAP.
Pain & opioidsAcetaminophen limit 4 g a day (counting combination products). Opioids: respiratory and CNS depression, constipation; itching is histamine, not allergy. Reversal: naloxone.
DementiaDonepezil (cholinesterase inhibitor) for mild to moderate Alzheimer's; memantine (NMDA blocker) for moderate to severe.

Endocrine

Dive deeper: NUR 325 diabetes · NUR 326 endocrine · NUR 406 endocrine emergencies

TopicHigh-yield points
InsulinRapid-acting (lispro): give with the meal tray. Regular is the only IV insulin (DKA, pumps). NPH is cloudy: draw clear before cloudy. Glargine: long-acting.
DKA vs HHSDKA: type 1, ketosis, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, anion gap acidosis; over about 24 h. HHS: type 2, older, no ketosis, glucose can exceed 1,000, builds over days, neuro changes. Treat: fluids first, potassium (serum normal or high but total body depleted), IV insulin with hourly glucose; add dextrose at 200–250. Most common trigger: infection.
ThyroidLevothyroxine on an empty stomach about 30 min before breakfast. Thyroid storm: fever 104–105, HR above 140, A-fib; beta blocker, PTU, then iodine 1 h later, steroids. Myxedema coma: hypothermia, bradycardia, hyponatremia, hypoventilation; IV levothyroxine + hydrocortisone, treat before labs.
Addison's & crisisThree S's: sugar, salt, sex. Low aldosterone: hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension, salt craving; low cortisol: hypoglycemia, hyperpigmentation. Lifelong hydrocortisone; never stop abruptly; 3 × 3 rule in stress; medical alert ID. Crisis: IV hydrocortisone, fluids, glucose.
Steroids (long term)The adrenal gland can't respond to stress, so the dose must increase when sick; taper, never stop abruptly.
SIADH vs DISIADH: too much ADH, water retained, dilutional hyponatremia. DI: too little ADH, large dilute urine output, hypernatremia.

Infection, Antimicrobials & Immunity

Dive deeper: NUR 325 immunity & infection · NUR 326 antimicrobials & HIV

TopicHigh-yield points
CephalosporinsCross-sensitivity with penicillin is rare (1–4%); avoid if the penicillin reaction was anaphylaxis. Ceftriaxone: once daily, crosses into the CSF.
VancomycinMRSA. IV: nephrotoxic (watch renal labs). PO: only for C. diff (stays in the gut).
Other antibioticsFluoroquinolones: CNS effects, seizures, prolonged QT, photosensitivity. Metronidazole: no alcohol 24 h before and at least 36 h after. Bactrim: first-line uncomplicated UTI; not with a sulfa allergy.
C. diffFrequent, very watery diarrhea after antibiotics (normal flora killed); diagnosed by stool toxin.
HIVDestroys CD4 cells; AIDS = CD4 below 200 or an AIDS-defining condition. Goal: undetectable viral load. PEP: ART for 28 days, then tests at 6 and 12 weeks.
CBC & neutropeniaBands up = shift to the left (acute bacterial infection). ANC below 1,000: neutropenic precautions (hand hygiene, no sick contacts, no raw produce or fresh flowers).
HypersensitivityType I: IgE and mast cells, about 15–20 min (anaphylaxis: epinephrine). Type IV: T cells, 48–72 h (TB skin test).

Hematology

Dive deeper: NUR 326 anemia · NUR 325 coagulation

TopicHigh-yield points
Anemia typesMicrocytic (small, pale): iron deficiency. Macrocytic (large): B12 or folate deficiency.
IronBlack or dark green stools are expected; liquid stains teeth (straw); don't crush coated tablets. IV iron dextran: test dose first (anaphylaxis).
Sickle cellCells sickle when oxygen is low; triggers include exertion and dehydration; painful crisis. Oxygen, hydration, pain control.
EpoetinFor anemia of CKD; only if Hgb below 10; check Hgb before each dose.

Renal, GI & Liver

Dive deeper: NUR 326 renal · NUR 326 liver & pancreas · NUR 326 GI · NUR 325 GI & urologic

TopicHigh-yield points
AKI & CKDAKI: pre-renal most common (blood loss, dehydration, sepsis). CKD: hyperkalemia (the big one), fluid overload, metabolic acidosis, anemia, uremia; GFR above 90 is normal.
PancreatitisSudden severe epigastric pain radiating to the back; amylase and lipase up; glucose up or down. Grey Turner (flank) and Cullen (umbilical) bruising = hemorrhagic.
LiverHepatic encephalopathy: confusion to coma. Lactulose traps ammonia in the gut (2–3 soft stools a day). Acetaminophen overdose: acetylcysteine.
Ulcers & acid drugsGastric pain 1–2 h after eating; duodenal 2–4 h. H. pylori is the most common infectious cause of gastritis. PPIs ("-prazole") are for short-term use: long-term risk of pneumonia, bone loss and hip fracture.
LaxativesBulk-forming (psyllium) is OK long term: take with plenty of water.

Cancer, Chemotherapy & Musculoskeletal

Dive deeper: NUR 326 cancer, chemo & MSK

TopicHigh-yield points
Chemo toxicitiesRapidly dividing cells: hair, GI (N/V, stomatitis), bone marrow. Highest infection and bleeding risk at the nadir (about 7–14 days). Extravasation: severe tissue damage.
Drug classesDoxorubicin: cardiotoxic (lifetime max dose). Vincristine: peripheral neuropathy; IV only (intrathecal is fatal). Cyclophosphamide: hemorrhagic cystitis. Methotrexate: nephrotoxic, hepatotoxic, category X.
OsteoporosisAlendronate: morning, sip of plain water, empty stomach, stay upright (esophageal ulcers). Raloxifene: clot risk, stop 72 h before a procedure.
FracturesCompartment syndrome: pain out of proportion, weak pulses: fasciotomy. Fat embolism after long-bone fracture: hypoxemia, altered LOC, petechial rash.
Arthritis & goutRA: symmetric, morning stiffness, systemic; methotrexate once weekly with folic acid. Gout: big toe; allopurinol prevents (no help in an attack); colchicine: vomiting = toxicity, stop it.

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

Antidote for heparin, and how do you give it?
Protamine sulfate, IV slowly.
Antidote for warfarin?
Vitamin K (or FFP). Keep vitamin K foods consistent.
Opioid overdose reversal?
Naloxone (Narcan).
Digoxin: what do you check and when do you hold?
Apical pulse a full minute; hold if below 60. Low potassium raises toxicity.
Chest pain not relieved 5 minutes after one SL nitro?
Call 911.
Why can't nitroglycerin be given with sildenafil?
Severe hypotension (it can be fatal).
Left vs right heart failure?
Left = lungs (crackles, orthopnea). Right = body (JVD, edema, weight gain, ascites).
Tamponade signs?
Hypotension, JVD, muffled heart sounds, pulsus paradoxus: pericardiocentesis.
ROME?
Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal (pH vs the causative value).
pH 7.30, PaCO2 50, HCO3 26?
Uncompensated respiratory acidosis.
Vomiting and NG suction cause which disorder?
Metabolic alkalosis.
The only asthma rescue drug?
SABA (albuterol).
Isoniazid supplement and why?
Vitamin B6 to prevent peripheral neuropathy.
Suspected stroke: first test?
Non-contrast CT: never anticoagulate a bleed.
Phenytoin level and classic teaching?
10–20 mcg/mL; gingival hyperplasia (good dental care).
Sinemet diet teaching?
Avoid high-protein meals (they decrease it).
Myasthenic vs cholinergic crisis?
Myasthenic = too little drug (improves with edrophonium). Cholinergic = too much (SLUDGE; antidote atropine).
GBS: what's the priority?
Breathing muscles: ascending weakness can need a ventilator.
Which insulin can go IV?
Regular.
DKA: what comes first?
IV fluids; check potassium before insulin.
Addison's patient gets an infection?
Increase the steroid (3 × 3 rule); never stop abruptly.
Thyroid storm: PTU and iodine order?
PTU first, iodine 1 hour later.
SIADH vs DI sodium?
SIADH: hyponatremia. DI: hypernatremia.
AIDS diagnosis?
CD4 below 200 or an AIDS-defining condition.
Cephalosporin with a penicillin allergy?
OK unless the reaction was anaphylaxis.
Metronidazole teaching?
No alcohol 24 h before and 36 h after.
Black stools on iron?
Expected, not a GI bleed.
Alendronate teaching?
Morning, plain water, empty stomach, upright.
Vincristine: hallmark effect and route?
Peripheral neuropathy; IV only (intrathecal is fatal).
Methotrexate for RA: how often?
Once weekly, with folic acid.
